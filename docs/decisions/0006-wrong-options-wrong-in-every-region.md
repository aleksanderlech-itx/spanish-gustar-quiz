# 0006. Wrong options must be wrong in every region

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-01
- Source: issue #69 (present perfect activity)

## Context

Choice questions show the answer and one wrong option. Some forms that look
like natural distractors are correct Spanish somewhere. In the present perfect
quiz, the preterite was the obvious wrong option for recent-past sentences, but
much of Latin America says hoy comí or ¿ya comiste? where Spain says hoy he
comido. Likewise, tener plus a participle is wrong as the perfect (tengo comido)
but correct as a resultative with a transitive verb (tengo preparada la cena).
Marking a correct regional or alternative form as wrong teaches the wrong
lesson and contradicts project rule 5 (neutral standard Spanish).

## Decision

- A wrong option must be wrong in every region and every reading of the
  sentence. If a form is standard anywhere, it cannot be the distractor.
- When a contrast is only wrong in some contexts, restrict the items to the
  contexts where it is always wrong. The present perfect "haber not tener"
  items use intransitive verbs only, so tener plus a participle has no valid
  reading.
- When a regional difference is the point of an item, it is flagged in the PR
  (rule 5), and the explanation names the region.

## Consequences

Some intuitive distractors are lost: the present perfect quiz has no preterite
options. Every new bank must check its distractors against regional usage
before review, and explanations or FAQ copy should say when an alternative is
also correct.
